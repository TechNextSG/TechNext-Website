Key takeaways

  • The BIR has not named Odoo in any issuance or list we could find, and it has not accredited or endorsed any e-invoicing provider yet (public advisory, 14 September 2026).
  • The BIR registers your business and your system, not a software brand: books of accounts through ORUS, and a Permit to Issue (PTI) for e-invoicing that names your approved system.
  • Covered taxpayers must issue electronic invoices by 31 December 2026 (RR 26-2025, RMC 98-2026).
  • Odoo's Philippine localisation already produces the 2307, SLSP, SAWT, QAP and 2550Q data from your own entries.

Most BIR questions we hear before an Odoo project start the same way: "Is Odoo BIR-approved?" The honest answer is that the BIR does not approve software brands the way people expect. It sets rules in Revenue Regulations (RR), explains them in Revenue Memorandum Circulars (RMC), and tells its own offices how to apply them in Revenue Memorandum Orders (RMO). Your system has to meet those rules, and your business registers it. This guide lists the issuances that matter for a company running its books in an ERP, and where Odoo fits.

Has the BIR said anything about Odoo?

No. We found no BIR issuance, advisory or published list that names Odoo or any Odoo partner. That includes registered CAS lists and anything about e-invoicing providers.

  • No accredited e-invoicing providers yet. In a public advisory dated 14 September 2026, the BIR said it has not accredited, authorised, certified, recognised or endorsed any entity as an e-invoicing service provider, and warned against claims such as "BIR-accredited" or "BIR-certified".
  • Accreditation rules now exist. RMC 110-2026 (12 October 2026) sets out the requirements for e-invoicing service providers to be accredited. No list of accredited providers had been published when we wrote this.
  • The permit is yours. Under RMC 98-2026 the BIR issues the PTI to the taxpayer, specifying the approved software or system. If you change or migrate systems, you need a new or amended PTI.

So treat any "BIR-accredited ERP" claim with care. What you can say accurately is: your business registers Odoo as its computerised books and uses it to apply for a PTI.

E-invoicing: who is covered by 31 December 2026

  • E-commerce and internet sellers that are small, medium or large. Micro taxpayers are exempt.
  • Taxpayers under the Large Taxpayers Service, and large taxpayers under RA 11976 and RR 8-2024.
  • Businesses on a CAS or CBA with electronic invoicing or other invoicing software.
  • Exporters, incentive-registered enterprises and POS users join later, through a separate regulation.

RMC 98-2026 is explicit that invoices made in Word, Excel or Google Sheets are not e-invoices, and that a system that only prints invoices is not e-invoicing. For the full checklist, read our BIR e-invoicing guide.

Where Odoo fits

Odoo's Philippine localisation produces the data for the recurring BIR reports from the entries you already post:

  • Withholding taxes with their ATC codes, and the 2307 per payee
  • SAWT and QAP as DAT files for the BIR's tools
  • SLSP as a DAT file, and the 2550Q VAT summary
  • Your TIN, branch code and RDO on the company record

Common questions

Is Odoo BIR-accredited? {{icon:chevron}}

No software brand is "BIR-accredited" in the way the question suggests. The BIR registers your books and issues your PTI for the system you use, and as of its 14 September 2026 advisory it had not accredited any e-invoicing service provider. Your business can register Odoo as its computerised books and use it for its PTI application.

What is the difference between an RR, an RMC and an RMO? {{icon:chevron}}

A Revenue Regulation (RR) sets binding rules under the Tax Code. A Revenue Memorandum Circular (RMC) explains or clarifies them for taxpayers. A Revenue Memorandum Order (RMO) tells BIR offices how to carry them out.

Do I still need to register my books if I use Odoo? {{icon:chevron}}

Yes. Books of accounts are registered through ORUS (RMC 3-2023 and RMC 4-2026), whatever system produces them.

Can Odoo produce my 2307 and SLSP? {{icon:chevron}}

Yes. The Philippine localisation produces 2307 data per payee and exports the SLSP, SAWT and QAP as DAT files for the BIR's tools, from the invoices and bills you post.
