Odoo Online, Odoo.sh or on-premise: which hosting fits?

Odoo runs in three ways: in Odoo's cloud, on Odoo's developer platform, or on servers you control. The right choice depends on one question more than any other: will you need custom code?

The short answer

  • Odoo Online suits most first phases: Odoo hosts everything, and there is no custom code.
  • Odoo.sh is for companies that need custom modules or code-level integrations, with staging branches built in.
  • On-premise is for companies whose IT policy or data rules require their own servers.

Choosing where Odoo runs is one of the first decisions in any project, and it is easy to get wrong in either direction: paying for infrastructure you don't need, or picking a setup that can't take the customisation you will need next year. TechNext implements Odoo on all three options, so here is how they compare in practice.

Odoo Online: Odoo's cloud

Odoo Online is Odoo's software-as-a-service. Odoo hosts the database, handles servers, backups and security, and runs version upgrades through its own upgrade service. You can configure everything Odoo offers, and on the Custom plan you can use Odoo Studio to add fields, views and automations without code.

What you cannot do is install custom Python modules or third-party modules outside what Odoo provides. For many companies that is fine: standard Odoo plus configuration covers far more than people expect. Odoo's One App Free and Standard plans run on Odoo Online.

Odoo.sh: Odoo's platform for custom code

Odoo.sh is Odoo's cloud platform for projects that need code. It connects to a GitHub repository, so custom modules are developed, reviewed and deployed like any software, and it gives you development and staging branches that copy production data for testing. Odoo still runs the infrastructure; you or your partner own the code. Odoo.sh needs the Custom plan, and hosting is billed on top of the subscription.

On-premise: your own servers

On-premise means running Odoo on your own servers or your own cloud account. You get full control of the environment, and full responsibility for it: hosting, backups, security patches, monitoring and upgrades. For Odoo Enterprise it needs the Custom plan, billed yearly. It makes sense when data-residency rules or internal IT policy require it, or when a company already runs its own infrastructure team.

How TechNext helps clients choose

We decide hosting during discovery, once we know what the first phase needs. Our rule of thumb:

  1. Start on Odoo Online if standard apps and configuration cover your processes. It is the simplest to run and the cheapest to start.
  2. Choose Odoo.sh as soon as a requirement needs custom code: a bespoke integration, a module Odoo does not have, or logic Studio cannot express.
  3. Go on-premise only for a concrete reason, such as a regulator, a group IT policy or existing infrastructure, because it moves the whole operations burden to you.

A company can move later. An Odoo Online database can be moved to Odoo.sh or on-premise when custom code becomes necessary, and we plan that move like a small project, tested on a copy first.

Common questions

Can we install custom modules on Odoo Online?

No. Odoo Online supports configuration and, on the Custom plan, Odoo Studio. Custom Python modules need Odoo.sh or an on-premise installation.

Which Odoo hosting option is cheapest?

Usually Odoo Online, because hosting is included in the subscription. Odoo.sh adds hosting fees on top of the Custom plan, and on-premise adds your own infrastructure and operations costs.

Can we move from Odoo Online to Odoo.sh later?

Yes. A database can move from Odoo Online to Odoo.sh or on-premise when you need custom code. TechNext plans and tests the move on a copy before switching.

Which hosting does TechNext recommend?

Odoo Online for most first phases, Odoo.sh when a requirement needs custom code, and on-premise only when IT policy or data rules require it.
