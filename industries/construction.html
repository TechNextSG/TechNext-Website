Odoo for contractors, in one paragraph

Odoo for construction runs each job as a project with its own budget, purchases, timesheets and progress billing, so you see cost to date against budget and invoice milestones without rebuilding spreadsheets. Site teams work from their phones, and in Odoo 20 even offline.

        Budget and plan: the budget of S$ 1,250,000 is spread over twelve months as the planned value (PV) S-curve; approved variation orders add to the contract and the budget.

        Earned value: each month, the percentage of work done times the budget gives the earned value (EV); actual cost (AC) comes from the purchases, subcontractor bills and timesheets on the project's analytic account. CPI is EV divided by AC, SPI is EV divided by PV, and the estimate at completion is the budget divided by CPI.

        Progress claims: each month the certified work done to date, less retention (5% or 10%, limited to 5% of the contract sum) and less previous claims, is invoiced with GST at 9%.

        Subcontractors and retention: subcontractor claims are certified and billed to the project, with retention held from them; the retention ledger shows what the client holds from us and what we hold from subcontractors, and when each is released.

        Sample project and figures. Odoo records the budget, costs, progress and invoices per project; earned value is a report we build on that data (for example an Odoo spreadsheet dashboard), and how retention is set up (a retention line and account, or payment terms) is agreed in discovery.

        Three problems we see first.

        • Costs found at the end

          Subcontractor claims and site purchases sit in email until the project closes; the margin is a surprise.

        • Progress billing by spreadsheet

          Certified percentages, retention and variations tracked in Excel, invoices typed from it.

        • Materials nobody tracks

          Deliveries straight to site, no receipt against the order, stock written off at year end.

        Odoo for contractors, answered.

        Can Odoo do job costing for construction?

        Yes. Each job runs as a project with an analytic account, so purchases, subcontractor bills and timesheets land against it, and the project overview shows cost to date against budget.

        Does Odoo support progress billing?

        Yes. Sales orders can be invoiced by milestone, so progress claims follow the contract, and variations can be added as new lines or quotations.

        Can site teams use Odoo without a signal?

        Odoo 20 adds an offline mode for creating and editing records, and field work now lives in the Planning app with a live map of technicians.

        Can we keep drawings and contracts in Odoo?

        Yes. Documents stores files against the project, and Sign collects signatures on subcontracts, variations and handover forms.

        Where does TechNext start with a contractor?

        With one live project: its budget, purchases, timesheets and first progress claim, run end to end before the next jobs move over.

        Is Odoo BIR-accredited?

        No software is BIR-accredited in that sense: the BIR registers your books and issues your Permit to Issue for the system you use, and its advisory of 14 September 2026 says it has not accredited any e-invoicing service provider. What matters is that your books, invoices and withholding meet BIR requirements. TechNext sets Odoo up for them and advises on what applies to your company.

        Can TechNext set up Odoo for BIR requirements for contractors in the Philippines?

        Yes. Our Taguig City team configures Odoo Accounting for CAS or CBA registration, 2307 certificates, SLSP, SAWT and QAP reports and, for covered taxpayers, e-invoicing preparation. Book a discovery call and an adviser reviews your current invoicing and books first.
